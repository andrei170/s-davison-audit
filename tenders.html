Half Your Turnover Comes From Twelve Councils. They All Publish In The Same Place, And Your Own Is One Of Them.

Prepared for Michael · 28 September 2026 · AG1 Consulting

You said you do about one tender a month. This is the list of places a tender can come from.

You told us roughly 50% council or government, about 30% engineering firms, and that anything over £10,000 has to go through a tender process. You also said the jobs you actually want are hospitals and new construction, and work that lasts three to six weeks.

None of that is a marketing problem. It is a question of whether you are seeing the opportunities when they are published, and whether you are registered where you need to be when they are.

Door 1 — NEPO, And Why It Matters Most To You
THE TWELVE NORTH EAST AUTHORITIES BUYING THROUGH NEPO South Tyneside <-- your own council Newcastle Gateshead North Tyneside Sunderland Durham Northumberland Darlington Hartlepool Middlesbrough Redcar & Cleveland Stockton-on-Tees Source: nepo.org/about, read 28 September 2026
The point is not that NEPO exists. It is that twelve buyers sit behind one door.

You are already doing council work. What you may not be doing is watching the other eleven. The same kind of job you win in South Tyneside — a school rewire, a distribution board replacement, a lighting contract — is being bought by Newcastle, Gateshead, Sunderland and Durham, all inside the fifty to seventy mile radius you said you work in.

That is the cheapest growth available to you, because it needs no new capability, no new staff and no new trade. It is the work you already do, for buyers of the same kind, who happen to sit in a different postcode and publish in a place you are not watching.

Door 2 — ProContract, Which Is Where NEPO Opportunities Actually Land

What you do Register the company once, set your categories and your radius, and let it notify you. Who does it Somebody in your office, once. It is not a monthly job.
This is the one to do first, and it is free

A portal you are not registered on cannot tell you anything. If the categories are not set against electrical installation and the geography is not set to your radius, the notifications never arrive and the opportunities pass without you ever seeing them.

We would do this with you, not for you. It is your company registration and it should stay in your name and your control — the same way we would tell you to keep ownership of your own website and your own Google listing.

These cover the whole country, so the filters matter more than on NEPO. Set them to your trade and your radius or you will get a hundred irrelevant alerts a week and stop reading them, which is worse than not having them.

We have not checked which specific opportunities are open today. That is the first thing we would do together rather than something to promise in a document.

Door 5 — The Hospital Route You Asked About
sbs.nhs.uk NHS Shared Business Services verified live 28 Sep 2026 crowncommercial.gov.uk now trading as the Government Commercial Agency -- verified 28 Sep 2026 HOW NHS ELECTRICAL WORK IS USUALLY BOUGHT Not job by job. Trusts buy through FRAMEWORKS -- you get onto the framework first, then compete for work released under it.
You said hospitals would be a good one to get into. This is the actual mechanism.

The reason hospital work feels closed off is that it usually is, until you are on a framework. Trusts are not generally putting individual jobs out to open tender — they are calling off against suppliers who were appointed in advance.

So the question is not “how do I win a hospital job”, it is “which framework covers electrical work in the North East, and when does it next open”. That is a slower door than NEPO, and it is worth opening precisely because it is slower — the firms who have not bothered are your competition.

We would find the specific framework and its renewal date rather than guess at it here.

This is why the website work and the tender work are the same job

Every item on that list is also the thing missing from your site. The school rewire at roughly £300,000, the sub-sea engineering client, the council work going back years — none of it is written down anywhere a buyer can read it.

So we are not proposing a website and separately proposing tender work. Writing up four or five real contracts properly gives you the evidence a PQQ asks for and fills the site at the same time. One piece of work, two uses.

And the certificate has to be fixed first, because a buyer who Googles you during a tender evaluation currently gets a browser warning. That is a bad first impression at the exact moment it costs most.

The Order We Would Do It In
  • Fix the certificate. Nothing else is worth doing while the front door throws a warning. You said you do not have the login details — we can tell you exactly what to ask GoDaddy for.
  • Register on ProContract and set the categories and radius. Free, once, and it turns eleven councils you are not watching into eleven councils that email you.
  • Write up four or five real contracts properly — value, duration, what was involved, outcome. This is the PQQ evidence and the case studies in one go.
  • Put the accreditation numbers on the site, not just the logos.
  • Then sector pages — local authority, education, healthcare, new build — so a buyer searching finds a page written for them rather than a general homepage.
  • Contracts Finder and Find a Tender alerts once the filters can be set sensibly.
  • The NHS framework question last, because it is the slowest and it depends on the evidence above existing first.

You do one tender a month. Eleven more councils buy what you sell.

None of this needs a new trade, a new van or a new member of staff.
