Competitor analysisYou beat both local rivals on paper — and lose to both, because your site does not open
The Short Version
  • Your SSL certificate has expired. Error code SEC_E_CERT_EXPIRED. Anyone typing sdavisonelectrical.com gets a full-page browser security warning instead of your website.
  • The site itself is completely fine. We bypassed the certificate check and it loaded normally. Nothing is hacked and nothing is lost — it is simply unreachable.
  • It expired on 16 May 2026 — 130 days ago. And it is a purchased RapidSSL certificate from DigiCert, not one of the free auto-renewing kind. Paid ones must be bought again; when the reminder goes to an old address, the site just stops opening one day in May.
  • Fix: renew it, or switch to a free auto-renewing certificate — most UK hosts including 20i provide those as standard. We would suggest the second, so it cannot recur.
  • You have GA4 running (G-BRQGM2ME5Z) — so once it is fixed you can look back and find the exact date traffic fell off. That tells you what this has cost.
  • Behind the warning is a better site than we first said. Five pages, 2,950 words — including a 1,099-word commercial page and a 1,112-word industrial page, and NICEIC + CHAS stated in full on your about page. That is more than either local rival publishes. (We corrected this — our first read said 307 words and thin.)
  • Which makes the certificate worse, not better. You have written 2,950 words about exactly the work you want more of, and nobody has read one of them since 16 May.
  • Companies House: S DAVISON (ELECTRICAL) LTD, 05595351, incorporated 18 October 2005, South Shields. Your site says “over 30 years” and the company dates from 2005 — both likely true, but say it explicitly (“trading since 19xx, incorporated 2005”) so a procurement officer never has to wonder.
  • You work commercial and industrial only, not domestic — so the website is not a lead source and we are not treating it as one. It is the page a buyer, main contractor or procurement officer opens when they check you out. That is what the certificate is costing you.
  • What a pre-qualification check wants and you do not publish: scheme registration numbers (you name NICEIC and CHAS but not the numbers), insurance cover levels, named sectors, named contracts, and a social value position. Thirty years of public and private work and not one contract written up.
  • Social value is often scored on public-sector awards. A thirty-year South Shields family firm employing local people has a good story there and tells it nowhere. Free to write down.
  • You beat both South Shields rivals on almost every measure — 307 words to their 182 and 195, four tappable numbers to one and none, the only enquiry form, the only analytics, the longest history. None of it counts while the site does not open.
  • But Schofield Electricals publishes four accreditations to your two — including Constructionline, the register a great many public-sector buyers use to pre-qualify suppliers. You work for local authorities and want more tender work. Worth knowing whether you hold it.
  • Do the certificate today. Everything else can wait. A warning page is worse than having no website at all.
