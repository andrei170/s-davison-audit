The Short Version
  • Your certificate is broken and it is the first thing to fix. We could not load your site over a normal secure connection at all. It only opened once we told our tools to ignore the certificate. A procurement officer checking a bidder will not click past that warning. Check GoDaddy first, you may already have paid for it.
  • You have the only working analytics in your market, alongside one competitor. Of seven firms, five have nothing or a dead tag. We owe you a correction here: on the call we said we found no Google tracking on your site. That was wrong. You have a live GA4 property. Our first automated read failed because of the certificate and reported an empty site.
  • Thirty years of contracts and not one written up. The £300,000 school rewire, the sub-sea engineering client, the large cable installations few local firms take on. None of it appears anywhere a buyer can see. On tendered work this is the evidence that decides who gets invited, and you raised it yourself on the call.
  • On Google Ads, you made a fair point and we went and checked it. You said nobody browses for a commercial electrician. AEL Electrical, working the same North East commercial ground, is running Google Ads with a conversion tag installed. They are measuring it and they have not switched it off. That does not prove it would work for you, but it means the question is open.
  • Half your work is council and government, and all twelve North East councils publish through one procurement portal. You bid about one tender a month. We do not know whether that is all that suits you or a fraction of what is published, and that is the first thing worth establishing.
  • NICEIC distinguishes nobody here, CHAS does. Six of seven firms name NICEIC. Only you and one other show CHAS, which is what public sector buyers ask about. But you state it without evidencing it — no registration numbers, no insurance cover levels. Those belong in the footer, on every page.
  • What is genuinely strong: your commercial page at 1,096 words and industrial at 1,109 are the best local content we found on either subject. Four tappable numbers. Clear commercial and industrial positioning with no domestic drift. Eight staff, thirty years, and a field where one competitor displays no accreditation at all.
  • One thing to think about before Monday: when you lose a tender, do you ever find out who won it?
