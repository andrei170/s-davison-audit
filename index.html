We rebuilt this document after our call. The first version was aimed at winning domestic enquiries through a form, and that is not your business.

This one is aimed at the people who actually buy from you: council and government procurement, engineering firms, and the person checking you out before a tender.

The headline finding has not changed, and it is still the first thing to fix. Your certificate is broken. We could not load your site over a normal secure connection at all. It only loaded once we told our tools to ignore the certificate.

Finding 1 — The Certificate, And Why It Is Worse For You Than For A Domestic Firm
Normal secure request to sdavisonelectrical.com failed — no response at all Same request, ignoring the certificate 200 OK, site loads fine
Nothing is broken behind it. The building is fine, the front door is jammed.

Your site works. The content is there, the pages load, nothing has been lost. The only problem is that a browser will not open it without showing a warning that says the connection is not private.

On domestic work you would lose some enquiries to this. On your work it is more serious than that. A procurement officer running pre-qualification checks on a bidder, or a facilities manager who has been given your name, will not click past a security warning on a company they are considering handing a contract to. It reads as a firm that does not look after its own house.

You mentioned you may already be paying GoDaddy for this. If the domain and email are with them, there is a reasonable chance the certificate is already bought and simply not applied. Check that before paying anybody, including us.

You told us it has been like this "probably two years". We can only confirm it is broken today.

We owe you a correction on this one, and it is in your favour

On our call we said we could not find any Google tracking on your site. That was wrong. You have a live Google Analytics 4 property and it has been running. The reason our first look missed it is the certificate: an automated read over a normal secure connection gets nothing back at all, so it reported an empty site. The written audit had it right; the spoken version did not.

Why this matters commercially. Two of six competitors have any working measurement. One of them, North East Electrical, is running Google's old analytics product which stopped collecting data in July 2023 — they have twenty-eight pages and twenty-eight enquiry forms and no idea which of them works.

So you are ahead, in a market where nearly nobody is counting. The frustrating part is that the thing doing the counting sits behind a door that will not open.

Finding 3 — Thirty Years Of Contracts And Not One Of Them Written Down
Named projects or case studies published: S Davison none Switch Electrical none found M E C (North East) none found North East Electrical 2 pages Your own examples, from our call: A school rewire, around £300,000 A sub-sea engineering client — ROV manufacture, power and generator installation Distribution circuits and large cable installation that few local firms take on
This is the largest gap in the document and it costs nothing but time to close

You told us there are not many firms locally doing the bigger cable installations you do. That is a genuine competitive position and there is no evidence of it anywhere a buyer can see.

Commercial and public-sector work is not bought the way domestic work is bought. A homeowner picks on price and on who answers the phone. A procurement officer has to justify the award, often in writing, to somebody above them. What they are looking for is evidence you have done this exact thing, at this size, before.

Three written up properly would put you ahead of every firm in this comparison bar one. Each needs the building and its use, what the job involved, the scale, how long it took, and how you worked around an occupied site. The school is the obvious first one.

You raised this yourself on the call. You said you want case studies on the site, and demonstrations of what you do. We agree, and we would put it above everything else on this list except the certificate.

Finding 4 — "Nobody Searches For A Commercial Electrician." One Of Your Competitors Disagrees, With Money.
Google Ads conversion tracking found on each firm: AEL Electrical AW-17594297886, with a conversion tag S Davison none Switch Electrical none M E C (North East) none North East Electrical none Retroflo none Electrical Solutions NE none
You made a fair point on the call and it deserves a real answer rather than a sales one

You said you could never understand how Google Ads would work for you, because somebody browsing is not looking for a commercial electrician, and that an industrial electrician is a specialist thing. That is a reasonable position and plenty of trades genuinely do not suit paid search.

So we checked instead of arguing. AEL Electrical, working the same North East commercial ground — fit-outs, schools, care facilities, factories — is running Google Ads with a conversion tag installed. The conversion tag is the part that matters. It means they are not just spending, they are measuring what the spend produces.

A firm that is measuring and has not switched it off is a firm for whom it is working. That is not proof it would work for you, and we are not going to pretend it is. It does mean the question is open rather than closed.

What we would actually suggest, and it is smaller than it sounds. Not a broad campaign. A narrow one against a handful of specific terms — the kind of thing a facilities manager types when a distribution board has failed or a building needs rewiring — geographically fenced to your travel radius, with a hard monthly cap. If it produces nothing in two months, it gets switched off and you have lost very little.

We have not seen AEL's account, spend or results. We observe only that the tags are present on their site.

Finding 5 — Half Your Work Comes From Councils. All Twelve Publish In One Place.
Your own figures, from our call: Council and government work ~50% of turnover Engineering companies ~30% Everything over £10,000 goes to tender Tenders you currently bid roughly one a month
This is the channel that actually matches how you sell, and it is not a website problem

The twelve North East councils advertise their opportunities through a single procurement body, NEPO, on its tendering portal. Framework agreements sit behind it, and getting onto a framework means being invited to bid rather than hunting for notices.

You told us you bid about one tender a month. We do not know whether that is because one is all that comes up that suits you, or because you are only seeing some of what is published. That is a genuine question and we are not going to guess the answer. It is the first thing we would want to establish on Monday.

Why we are raising it at all: you asked on the call where the jobs would actually come from. For a firm that is half public sector and where everything worth having goes to tender, the honest answer is that the largest single lever is probably not advertising. It is being visible, credible and registered where your buyers are already legally obliged to publish.

And that loops back to Finding 3. Frameworks and pre-qualification ask for evidence of comparable work at comparable value. The school rewire is exactly that, and it is written down nowhere.

Finding 6 — Your Accreditations Are Stated But Not Evidenced
Accreditations named on each firm's own site: S Davison NICEIC, CHAS Retroflo NICEIC, CHAS AEL Electrical NICEIC, Constructionline Switch Electrical NICEIC M E C (North East) NICEIC North East Electrical NICEIC Electrical Solutions NE none found On your site: Registration numbers not shown Insurance cover levels not shown
NICEIC is table stakes here. CHAS is where you separate.

Every firm we checked bar one names NICEIC. It does not distinguish you. CHAS does — only you and Retroflo show it, and it is a health and safety assessment that public sector buyers and main contractors specifically ask about.

But naming a scheme is not the same as evidencing it. Your registration numbers are not published and neither are your insurance cover levels. Those are the two things a procurement form asks for by name, and putting them in the footer means they appear on every page rather than being requested by email.

AEL carries Constructionline, which you do not show. Worth a conversation about whether you hold it, because it appears on a great many public sector pre-qualification requirements.

Finding 7 — No Sector Pages, And No Structured Data
Your two strongest pages: /industrial 1,109 words /commercial 1,096 words Homepage 305 words About 294 words Sector pages (local authority, education, healthcare, industrial client types) none Structured data: S Davison none found M E C (North East) FAQ, address, opening hours, coordinates North East Electrical LocalBusiness, reviews, ratings, breadcrumbs
Your two best pages are better than anything local. They are also doing all the work alone.

Your commercial and industrial pages are over a thousand words each and they are the strongest thing on the site. Your homepage is three hundred. The depth is in the right place, but there is nothing underneath it.

You named the sectors you want on the call: hospitals, new construction, schools, and the engineering and sub-sea clients you already serve. None of those has a page. A page per sector, written for that buyer, is what gets found by somebody searching for a contractor with experience in their specific setting.

Two competitors publish structured data and you publish none. That is the code that tells Google what kind of business you are, where you are and when you are open, rather than leaving it to guess from your words. It is a one-off job, not an ongoing cost.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Fix the certificate. Today if possible, and check GoDaddy first in case you have already paid for it. Nothing else on this list matters while the front door is jammed.
  • Write up three contracts. The school, the sub-sea client, and one large cable installation. This is the item that wins tenders and pre-qualifications, and it is the one no competitor except North East Electrical has bothered with.
  • Put registration numbers and insurance cover in the footer. Twenty minutes, appears on every page, and it answers the questions a procurement form asks by name.
  • Establish what you are actually seeing on the tender side. One bid a month may be all that suits you, or it may be a fraction of what is published. We should find out before deciding anything else.
  • Build a page per sector — local authority, education, healthcare, industrial and new build. Your commercial and industrial pages prove you can write them.
  • Add structured data. One-off job, and two of your competitors already have it.
  • Only then, a narrow paid search test, capped and geo-fenced, against a handful of specific terms. If it produces nothing in two months, switch it off.
