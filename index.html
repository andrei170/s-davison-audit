Finding 1 — What Actually Happens When Somebody Visits
http://sdavisonelectrical.com -> 301 redirect to https://sdavisonelectrical.com/ https://sdavisonelectrical.com -> connection fails -> SEC_E_CERT_EXPIRED "The received certificate has expired" With the certificate check bypassed: -> 200 OK — the site is there and works
In plain English

Your domain sends every visitor to the secure version of your site, and the security certificate on it has run out. Browsers will not show a site with an expired certificate. They show a red or grey warning page saying the connection is not private, with your domain name on it.

Most people close the tab at that point. A few click through the warning. Nobody tendering work reads a browser security warning and thinks “this is a firm I should invite to quote.”

The site itself is untouched and working. We bypassed the certificate check and it loaded normally. Nothing has been hacked and nothing is lost.

Finding 2 — This Is Usually A Ten-Minute Fix
And it costs nothing on most hosting

Your site is hosted with 20i, a UK host. Like almost every host now, they provide free certificates that normally renew automatically. When one expires it is usually because auto-renewal was switched off, the certificate was issued manually once, or a renewal quietly failed and nobody saw the email.

Whoever manages your hosting can reissue it from the control panel. On most setups it is one button and it takes effect within minutes.

This is the single most urgent thing in this document and it should be done before anything else is discussed. Every day it stays broken is a day your website is worse than not having one — because a warning page is worse than a blank.

Finding 3 — You Have Been Collecting Data On A Site Nobody Can Reach
Google Analytics 4 G-BRQGM2ME5Z installed and running Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found
There is a silver lining here

You have analytics installed. That means once the certificate is fixed, you will be able to see immediately whether traffic recovers — and roughly when it fell off.

It is worth looking at that history on the call. If there is a cliff in the traffic, that is the date the certificate expired, and it tells you exactly how long this has been costing you.

Finding 4 — Behind The Warning, The Site Is Thin
Homepage word count 307 Tappable phone numbers 4 Enquiry form 1 NICEIC mentioned yes CHAS mentioned yes "Commercial" 6 mentions "Industrial" 6 mentions Structured data none found
The right things are said, in not enough words

You state the two things that matter on commercial and industrial work — NICEIC approval and CHAS — and you say “over 30 years” and “public and private sector”. That is exactly what a buyer is looking for.

But the homepage is 307 words. A facilities manager or a council procurement officer checking you out gets a warning page, and if they push past it, three paragraphs.

You told Lewis you want more commercial and industrial tender work. That work is won on evidence: scheme numbers, insurance levels, named sectors, previous contracts. None of that is published.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Fix the certificate today. Before this call ends, if possible. It is the only item on this list that is actively costing you money every hour.
  • Look at the analytics history and find the date traffic fell. That tells you what this has cost.
  • Set a calendar reminder for the renewal, or confirm auto-renew is on. This should never happen twice.
  • Build the tender evidence out — NICEIC registration number, CHAS status, insurance cover levels, sectors served, and a handful of named contract types. That is what gets a firm onto a list.
  • Add LocalBusiness and Service structured data, and take the homepage past 307 words.
  • Then, and only then, talk about generating enquiries. There is no point driving anybody to a site that does not open.
