This compares you against firms doing what you do: commercial fit-outs, industrial installation, schools, care facilities, factories and distribution work across the North East.

The short version: this is a weak field on everything except content volume, and the one firm behaving like it intends to grow is the one spending on search.

Finding 1 — AEL Is The Only Firm Behaving Like It Wants To Grow
AEL Electrical (aelelectrical.co.uk) Google Ads tag AW-17594297886 Conversion tracking present Google Analytics 4 live Accreditations NICEIC, Constructionline Ground they work commercial projects, fit-outs, schools, care facilities, factories
This is the finding that answers the question you raised on the call

You said you could never understand how Google Ads would work for your business, because an industrial electrician is a specialist thing and people are not browsing for one. It is a fair argument and we did not have an answer for it at the time.

A firm on your patch, selling to your buyers, is spending money on it right now. And critically, they have a conversion tag installed, which means they can see what the spend returns. Somebody who is measuring and has not stopped is somebody for whom it is working.

That is not proof it would work for you. We have not seen their account, their spend or their results, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. But it moves this from "that could never work in our trade" to "somebody nearby appears to have made it work, and we should find out how."

They also carry Constructionline, which you do not show. On public sector pre-qualification that appears constantly, and it is the one credential in this table you are behind on.

Finding 2 — Switch Publishes Thirty Pages And Measures None Of Them
Switch Electrical & Mechanical (switchelectrical.uk) Pages 30 Words ~35,000 Technical content a large "info portal" — earthing, touch voltage, EV charger fault protection Analytics none found Structured data none found Tappable numbers 1 Named projects none found
They are doing the hardest part well and getting nothing back from it

Their technical library is genuinely good and it is real effort. Articles on automatic disconnection of supply, touch voltage, open PEN fault protection. That is content written by someone who knows the trade, and it is exactly the sort of thing that gets a firm taken seriously by an engineer.

They have no analytics at all, so they cannot tell you whether a single one of those pages has produced a phone call. And with one tappable number across thirty pages, a reader who is convinced has to work to contact them.

The lesson for you is not to copy the volume. It is that thirty pages without measurement is thirty pages of guesswork, and you already have the measurement they lack.

Finding 3 — North East Electrical Is The Only Firm Publishing Its Work
North East Electrical (northeastelectrical.co.uk) Pages 28 Named project pages 2 Structured data LocalBusiness, reviews, ratings, breadcrumbs Enquiry forms 28 Tappable numbers 4 Analytics Universal Analytics only — dead since July 2023
The best-presented site in the comparison, flying completely blind

They publish reviews and ratings as structured data, which is what produces the stars under a Google result. They have a form on every page and four tappable numbers. On presentation they beat everybody here, including you.

And their only analytics is Google's old product, which stopped collecting data in July 2023. They have twenty-eight forms and no working way of knowing which one anybody fills in. That tag has been sitting there doing nothing for over three years and nobody has noticed.

They are also positioned domestic-first — fuse boxes, consumer units, home electrics. They have a commercial page, but their centre of gravity is not your market. That separation is worth something to you: when a facilities manager lands on your site, your commercial and industrial pages tell them within a line that you do their kind of work.

Finding 4 — NICEIC Is Table Stakes. CHAS Is Where You Separate.

Six of seven name NICEIC, so it distinguishes nobody

Only you and Retroflo display CHAS. It is a health and safety assessment that public sector buyers and main contractors ask about by name, and on work that is half council-funded it carries more weight than NICEIC does.

The problem is that you state it and do not evidence it. No registration numbers, no insurance cover levels, nowhere on the site. Those are the exact fields a pre-qualification questionnaire asks for, and putting them in your footer means they sit on every page instead of being chased by email.

One firm in this comparison displays no accreditation at all, which tells you how low the floor is here. You are above it. You are just not proving it.

What This Field Tells Us To Do
  • Fix the certificate before anything else. You are the only firm here whose site a buyer cannot open, and every other advantage in this document is behind that door.
  • Write up the school, the sub-sea client and a large cable install. One competitor publishes named work. On tendered and pre-qualified business, that is the gap that decides invitations.
  • Put your registration numbers and insurance cover in the footer. You already hold the credential that separates you here; you are simply not proving it.
  • Ask AEL's question, not their answer. Find out what a narrow, capped, geo-fenced search test actually returns over two months before forming a view on whether paid search suits this trade.
  • Do not copy Switch's thirty pages. They have volume and no measurement. You have measurement and better commercial pages than any of them. Build sector pages instead, and count what they do.
  • Consider Constructionline. It is the one credential in this table you are behind on and it appears on a great many public sector requirements.

This is a weak field and you are already near the top of it on the things that are hard to buy.

Thirty years, eight staff, CHAS, the best commercial pages locally and working analytics. Behind a door that will not open.
