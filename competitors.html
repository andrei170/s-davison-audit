Constructionline is the one to ask about

You told Lewis you work for local authorities and want more of that kind of tender work. Constructionline is the register a great many public-sector buyers and main contractors use to pre-qualify suppliers — it exists specifically to be that check.

Your nearest competitor publishes it. You do not.

Two possibilities, and they lead different places. If you hold it and simply have not put it on the site, that is a twenty-minute fix once the site is reachable. If you do not hold it, that is worth more than everything else in both documents — because it may be the difference between being on a buyer's list and never appearing.

Requirements vary by buyer and contract, so we cannot tell you it is mandatory for your market. We can tell you the firm down the road decided it was worth having and worth displaying.

Where You Can Win
This is a weak field and you are already the best-equipped firm in it
  • Nobody here measures anything except you. Neither competitor has analytics of any kind. You have GA4 already running — you are the only one who will be able to see what works.
  • Nobody here has an enquiry form. You do. On commercial work, where a facilities manager would rather send details than explain them on a call, that matters.
  • Schofield publishes 182 words across 14 pages. That is a thin site with a lot of URLs. There is no depth in this market to beat — only a gate to get through.
  • Nobody is advertising or retargeting. No pixels, no ad tags, anywhere in the field.
  • You have the longest stated history — 30+ years against 1983 and 20+.
And where Schofield is genuinely ahead

Four accreditations to your two, including the two that matter most for public-sector pre-qualification. They have decided that being provably safe to hire is the product, and they lead with it.

They also publish 14 pages to your three. Thin pages — but a buyer searching for a specific service finds a page for it.

What We Could Not Check
Marked honestly rather than guessed
  • Which accreditations any firm here actually holds, including you. We read websites, not registers. A firm may hold more than it displays, or display on a page we did not read.
  • Inner pages on all three sites. We read homepages and sitemaps.
  • Whether either competitor appears on any tender list or framework, and whether you do. That is not visible from a website.
  • Everyone's Google Business Profile, reviews and map position, including yours.
  • Anyone's revenue, staffing, pricing, win rate or insurance levels, including yours.
  • Every commercial electrical contractor in Tyne and Wear. This is the set a search returned and we could verify — not a census, and it excludes the national M&E contractors bidding the largest work.
